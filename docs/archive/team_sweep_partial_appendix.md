## Atlanta Falcons Offense — 2026 Training Camp / Offseason Fantasy Football Report

**New regime: Kevin Stefanski takes over as head coach, Tommy Rees calling plays**

Atlanta hired Kevin Stefanski (formerly Browns HC) as head coach for 2026, with Tommy Rees installed as offensive coordinator/play-caller. This is a significant scheme change worth monitoring for how touches/target shares shift (Stefanski's Cleveland offenses leaned heavily on a workhorse back and play-action).

*Fantasy relevance:* New scheme could alter usage patterns for Bijan Robinson, London, and Pitts; worth tracking joint practice/preseason usage to see how touches are distributed before Week 1.


**QB competition: Falcons signed veteran Tua Tagovailoa; Tua expected to win Week 1 job over injured Michael Penix Jr.**

Atlanta signed Tua Tagovailoa to a one-year deal in free agency, setting up an offseason-long QB battle with 2024 first-rounder Michael Penix Jr. Penix tore his ACL in November 2025 and had season-ending reconstructive surgery; he's been limited to individual/7-on-7 drills to open camp (no full-team or contact work) and said full clearance could still be ~4 weeks away. Reporting (Falcoholic, SportsTalk ATL) now says it's 'highly unlikely' Penix is ready for Week 1, and multiple outlets project Tua as the Week 1 starter vs. Pittsburgh, with Penix a potential in-season replacement if Tua struggles. Cooper Rush was also signed as a veteran arm/insurance, and UDFA Jack Strand has taken some camp reps.

*Fantasy relevance:* Tua projects as the Week 1 starter and likely early-season fantasy-relevant passer feeding London/Pitts; Penix is a stash/dynasty watch whose value depends on Tua's performance and his own recovery pace. This is the single biggest swing factor for the passing-game hierarchy.


**Bijan Robinson signs record 3-year, up to $75M extension after training-camp hold-in — clear bell-cow entrenched**

Bijan Robinson held in (attended camp but didn't practice) to start camp seeking a new deal; Atlanta and Robinson agreed to a 3-year extension worth up to $75M, making him the NFL's highest-paid running back. He's coming off a career year (2,298 scrimmage yards — 1,478 rushing/820 receiving — most ever by a Falcon) and is entrenched as the workhorse. He has since ended the hold-in and returned to practice.

*Fantasy relevance:* Robinson remains a clear top-tier bell-cow RB1 for fantasy with a new long-term financial commitment signaling continued heavy usage under Stefanski/Rees.


**Tyler Allgeier left in free agency; Brian Robinson Jr. signed as new backup/complementary back**

Tyler Allgeier departed to the Arizona Cardinals in free agency. Atlanta replaced him by signing Brian Robinson Jr. (former Washington 3rd-rounder, most recently backup to Christian McCaffrey in San Francisco in 2025 — 92 carries/400 yards). He projects as Bijan's primary backup/complementary/short-yardage piece rather than part of an even committee. Third-string RB job is a camp battle among Nathan Carter, Tyler Goodson, and UDFA Cash Jones; Carlos Washington Jr. also departed.

*Fantasy relevance:* This is not a true committee — Bijan is the clear lead/bell-cow, with Brian Robinson Jr. as handcuff/change-of-pace and mostly touchdown-vulture/injury-insurance value. Worth a late-round handcuff pick in deeper leagues.


**WR room overhauled: Darnell Mooney, KhaDarel Hodge, Ray-Ray McCloud, and David Sills V are gone; Jahan Dotson and Olamide Zaccheaus signed, Zachariah Branch drafted**

The Falcons released Darnell Mooney in the offseason (cap-driven cut, reportedly saving cap space) along with KhaDarel Hodge, David Sills V, and Ray-Ray McCloud. To replace that depth, Atlanta signed free agents Jahan Dotson (ex-Eagles/Commanders) and Olamide Zaccheaus, and used a 3rd-round pick on explosive USC/Georgia transfer WR Zachariah Branch. Drake London remains the unquestioned, extended WR1. Camp competition for WR2/WR3 is between Dotson, Zaccheaus, and returning depth pieces Chris Blair and Dylan Drummond, with young players (Anthony, Brockington, Marion, Wells) fighting for roster spots.

*Fantasy relevance:* Drake London is a locked-in WR1 target hog regardless of QB. Behind him, the WR2 job is unsettled — Dotson and Zaccheaus are the leading combined committee bet — making that spot a late-round dart-throw rather than a clear fantasy asset. Some conflicting/aggregator reports still list Mooney as active, but the more current, official team-site preview (dated for 2026 camp) confirms Mooney's departure and the Dotson/Zaccheaus additions — treat those as the reliable read.


**Rookie WR Zachariah Branch (3rd round) drawing early praise; special-teams role secured, offensive role still developing**

Branch, described as having elite speed/playmaking instincts, is considered one of the steals of the 2026 draft class per early camp buzz. He's expected to lock in a kick-return role immediately and has flashed in camp (including reportedly catching a TD from Michael Penix Jr. in a limited full-team period), but an every-down offensive role is still being earned amid a crowded WR room.

*Fantasy relevance:* Branch is a name to stash in dynasty/deep redraft leagues as a big-play/return-TD threat with upside if he forces more snaps as camp progresses; not yet a clear-cut fantasy starter.


**Kyle Pitts signed a new 3-year, ~$54M extension after being franchise-tagged — trade rumors resolved for now**

After being franchise-tagged and drawing persistent trade rumors (multiple teams reportedly inquired about his availability), Kyle Pitts and the Falcons agreed to a new 3-year extension worth roughly $54M, keeping him in Atlanta. Some older aggregator posts still float 'Pitts trade' possibilities, but the extension (reported by NFL Network, CBS Sports, and the Falcons' own site) is the more recent and authoritative development.

*Fantasy relevance:* Pitts remains part of the core passing-game weapons (London, Pitts, Bijan) even with QB uncertainty, and the new deal removes the trade overhang that was clouding his outlook.


**No confirmed PUP-list Falcons offensive players beyond Penix's practice limitations**

Reporting to date centers on Michael Penix Jr. being limited (individual/7-on-7 only, not confirmed full PUP-list placement per available reports) as he continues ACL rehab, plus Bijan Robinson's contract-related hold-in (not an injury). No other Falcons offensive skill-position players were found reported on PUP or with notable camp injuries in the sources reviewed.

*Fantasy relevance:* Penix's practice status is the injury story to track; a PUP designation or setback would further push back his 2026 fantasy relevance, while progressing off it could accelerate a potential midseason QB change.

## Arizona Cardinals Offense - 2026 Training Camp / Fantasy Football Outlook

**Cardinals fired HC Jonathan Gannon, hired Mike LaFleur; Nathaniel Hackett running the offense**

After a 3-14 season, Arizona fired Jonathan Gannon and hired Mike LaFleur as head coach for 2026. Nathaniel Hackett is calling plays/serving as OC. LaFleur's scheme is described as motion-heavy, and the staff has emphasized multi-tight-end formations, which affects usage projections across the skill positions.

*Fantasy relevance:* New scheme/staff changes target distribution assumptions; motion-heavy, multi-TE looks could boost Trey McBride's already elite role and affect WR route diversity.


**Kyler Murray released; Jacoby Brissett is the new starting QB**

The Cardinals released longtime starter Kyler Murray in March 2026 (he drew interest from the Vikings and other teams as a backup option). Arizona re-signed/re-worked a deal with Jacoby Brissett (2-year, ~$9.2M 2026 cap hit) after a brief camp holdout over money; the two sides reached a new contract and he reported to camp. Brissett is the presumed Week 1 starter, coming off a strong 2025 relief stint (3,366 yards, 23 TD in 12 starts) that included good chemistry with Trey McBride. Gardner Minshew II (1-year, $8.25M) is the veteran backup, and third-round rookie Carson Beck (Miami) and Kedon Slovis round out the QB room. Beck has flashed some big plays (TD passes to rookie WR Reggie Virgil) but also turnover issues (2 INTs in one camp practice) and isn't expected to see real playing time in 2026 barring injury.

*Fantasy relevance:* Brissett is a fringe streaming/backup fantasy QB in a Murray-less offense; expect a more game-manager, run-leaning approach that could suppress some ceiling for WRs but is fine for pass-catching specialists like McBride. Watch Beck as a stash only in dynasty.


**Rookie Jeremiyah Love (drafted 3rd overall) is the new backfield centerpiece, pushing James Conner into a lesser role**

Arizona stunned many by taking Notre Dame RB Jeremiyah Love with the No. 3 overall pick in the 2026 draft (he signed with $53M guaranteed) despite already having James Conner and re-signing/free-agent-adding Tyler Allgeier (2yr/$12.25M) this offseason. GM Monti Ossenfort said the goal 'picking that high' was adding the most impactful player available. Multiple camp reports have Love atop the RB depth chart with Allgeier and Conner behind him, though OC Nathaniel Hackett publicly declined to name a starter, saying only 'he's definitely a rookie' about Love while praising Conner's return from foot surgery. Love has reportedly been 'cooking' as a pass-catcher in camp as well.

*Fantasy relevance:* This is the single biggest story for Cardinals fantasy value in 2026: Love profiles as a rookie RB1/lead-back breakout candidate (early ADP riser), while Conner's fantasy stock has cratered from lead-back to committee/change-of-pace/short-yardage role. Allgeier also cuts into Conner's/Benson's touches. Not a clean bell-cow situation yet -- camp battle for exact touch split is ongoing, but Love is the presumptive lead with real workhorse upside if he wins the job outright.


**Trey Benson has fallen to RB4/roster bubble; dealt with a knee flare-up**

2024 third-round pick Trey Benson, who had his 2025 season ended after 4 games by a meniscus injury, is now buried at RB4 behind Love, Allgeier, and Conner. He also missed a camp practice with a 'flare-up' in his surgically repaired left knee (called day-to-day, 'just a little discomfort' per LaFleur), which hurts his case in a crowded room. Veteran Bam Knight (re-signed) offers competing special-teams value at RB5, and there's chatter Benson could even be a trade candidate (e.g., speculation about Green Bay) if he doesn't perform in preseason.

*Fantasy relevance:* Benson has cratered from a potential preseason sleeper into a real roster-cut/trade risk; not fantasy relevant in most formats unless he lands elsewhere with a clearer path.


**PUP list at camp opening included TE Tip Reiman (ankle) and LT Paris Johnson Jr. (knee, called minor/maintenance)**

Arizona placed four players on the Reserve/PUP list to start camp: OLB Josh Sweat (knee), CB Garrett Williams (Achilles), TE Tip Reiman (ankle), and LT Paris Johnson Jr. (knee -- LaFleur called it 'honestly nothing... just maintenance,' expected back soon). Notably, RBs James Conner and Trey Benson were reported fully healthy and practicing at the time of the PUP announcement (Benson's knee issue arose later).

*Fantasy relevance:* Reiman's PUP stint keeps him firmly the TE2 behind Trey McBride with no real fantasy impact either way; Paris Johnson's knee is a swing-tackle-health storyline that matters more for offensive line/QB protection than direct fantasy value.


**WR1 job is a genuine open competition: Michael Wilson trending ahead of Marvin Harrison Jr. amid MHJ drop issues**

Multiple camp reports (Arizona Sports/Burn City Sports) describe Michael Wilson -- coming off a 1,000-yard 2025 season -- as 'Mr. Consistency' and trending up in the WR1/Z-receiver competition, benefiting from LaFleur's motion-heavy scheme. Meanwhile Marvin Harrison Jr. (No. 4 overall pick in 2024) has battled a 'case of the drops' in camp (9 drops on 189 targets over his first two NFL seasons per the reporting), raising questions ahead of his 2026 fifth-year-option decision. However, a later camp report notes Harrison 'rebounded' with improved consistency and a highlight diving catch from Brissett, suggesting the drops narrative was cooling by early August. Harrison's 2025 season was marred by a concussion (Wk 6), appendicitis/surgery (Wk 9), and bilateral heel injuries that limited him to 12 games (41-608-4, no 100-yard games) before he closed the year on IR; he's expected back at full health for 2026.

*Fantasy relevance:* Real uncertainty at the top of the WR corps -- Wilson looks like a safer PPR bet right now, while Harrison remains the higher-ceiling, boom/bust dynasty/redraft asset if he stays healthy and cuts down drops. Neither has clearly 'won' the job as of the most recent reports; volume could end up fairly split given LaFleur's scheme.


**WR3 battle has reportedly been won by re-signed veteran Zay Jones over Greg Dortch**

Zay Jones (re-signed to a 1-year, $4.25M deal after his 2025 season ended early on a torn Achilles suffered vs. Seattle) has 'decisively won' the WR3 competition over Greg Dortch, per SI's camp coverage -- dominating first-team reps and beating top corner Will Johnson in 1-on-1s. Kendrick Bourne was also signed in free agency (2yr/$11.47M) and is part of the top WR grouping alongside Wilson and Harrison per the team's own 'Roster Reset' piece, while Xavier Weaver, Devin Duvernay (also a returner), and Simi Fehoko round out depth.

*Fantasy relevance:* Jones projects as a low-end WR4/5 flex-only option if he stays healthy off the Achilles; Bourne offers change-of-pace slot/red-zone flashes but is buried behind Wilson/Harrison/Jones on target share.


**Rookie 5th-rounder Reggie Virgil (Texas Tech) is flashing as a legit role-earner**

Fifth-round pick Reggie Virgil has been one of the standout camp performers, described as playing 'faster than his 40 time,' with a highlight one-handed, OBJ-style catch and multiple touchdown connections with backup QB Carson Beck, plus special-teams value. The team's own roster-reset piece flagged him as a player 'to monitor' for a role under LaFleur.

*Fantasy relevance:* Deep-league/dynasty stash only in 2026, but worth knowing as a name climbing the WR6/7 depth chart with special-teams path to a roster spot and speed to eventually threaten for snaps.


**Trey McBride remains the unquestioned focal point of the passing game after record extension**

McBride signed a 4-year, $76 million extension (through 2029) making him the highest-paid TE in NFL history at the time, coming off a Pro Bowl 2025 season. He's firmly entrenched as Arizona's top receiving weapon and had strong camp reports (quickly returning to score after a hard collision in 11-on-11s). Backup TE spot features Tip Reiman (currently on PUP with an ankle injury) and free-agent addition Teagan Quitoriano (1-year deal).

*Fantasy relevance:* McBride remains a locked-in top-3 fantasy TE target regardless of the Cardinals' QB uncertainty, given the scheme's multi-TE tendencies and his central role in the passing game.


**Offensive line overhaul via free agency/draft could affect run-game and pass-pro efficiency**

Arizona revamped its guard spots, signing Isaac Seumalo (3yr/$31.5M, LG) and Matt Pryor (1yr, depth/competition), drafted G Chase Bisontis in Round 2 (competing at RG with Isaiah Adams, whose situation is complicated by off-field legal issues), and re-signed OT Elijah Wilkinson (2yr) to compete for the starting RT job against UDFA Josh Fryar. Starting LT Paris Johnson Jr. is dealing with a minor/maintenance knee issue (on PUP to open camp) but is expected back soon.

*Fantasy relevance:* Line uncertainty (especially at RT and interior) is a real downside risk for both the run-game efficiency (impacting Love/Conner/Allgeier) and Brissett's pass protection, worth monitoring into preseason.

## Baltimore Ravens 2026 Training Camp - Offense (Fantasy Football Outlook)

**New coaching staff overhauling the offense**

John Harbaugh was let go after the 2025 season and the Ravens hired former Chargers DC Jesse Minter as head coach, with Declan Doyle as the new offensive coordinator. Lamar Jackson has praised the new staff as 'a breath of fresh air' and said Doyle's play-calling feel has been easy to pick up. The team is reportedly planning to increase 11-personnel (3-WR) usage.

*Fantasy relevance:* New scheme creates some uncertainty in early usage patterns, but early camp reports on the passing game are positive.


**Lamar Jackson healthy after injury-plagued 2025, clear starter**

Jackson dealt with a hamstring injury (originating Week 3 vs. Detroit) and a back injury/contusion in 2025 that limited his rushing burst and cost him multiple starts (Cooper Rush and Tyler Huntley each started games in relief). He called last season a 'once-in-a-lifetime' bad stretch and says he 'couldn't really burst.' In camp he looks 'faster' and energized under the new staff. The Ravens released Cooper Rush and re-signed Tyler Huntley (2-yr, $11M) as the clear backup QB.

*Fantasy relevance:* Jackson remains the unquestioned starter and a top fantasy QB if healthy; his rushing volume/burst recovery is the key thing to track through preseason after last year's dip.


**Derrick Henry is the unquestioned bell-cow RB1 — no committee at the top**

Henry, now 32, remains firmly entrenched as the workhorse. He led the NFL in rushing attempts and yards over his two seasons in Baltimore and closed 2025 with four straight 100+ yard games. Rumors about the Ravens trading for Seattle's Kenneth Walker III as a Henry replacement were confirmed to be fan/opinion speculation, not real front-office activity.

*Fantasy relevance:* Henry should be drafted as a true bell-cow RB1; the backfield is not a timeshare at the top of the depth chart.


**Backfield pecking order set behind Henry: Hill locked in at RB2, Ali vs. Randall for RB3**

Justice Hill, who missed time late in 2025 with a neck injury, looked fully recovered in OTAs/minicamp and is the clear No. 2 back (runner/receiver/blocker). The real camp battle is for the No. 3 job between Rasheen Ali (special-teams background) and rookie Adam Randall (Clemson, converted from WR), which will likely be settled in preseason games.

*Fantasy relevance:* Hill has standalone flex/handcuff value in PPR as the clear backup; Ali/Randall have minimal fantasy relevance unless Henry is hurt.


**Zay Flowers is the clear WR1; Rashod Bateman needs a bounce-back as WR2**

Flowers is coming off an explosive 2025 (86 catches, 1,211 yards, 14.1 YPC) and is the unquestioned top target, projected for ~95 catches/1,300 yards in 2026. Bateman held onto the WR2 role but was a major disappointment in 2025 (just 19 catches, 224 yards) and faces real pressure to produce.

*Fantasy relevance:* Flowers is a locked-in WR1 fantasy asset; Bateman is a boom/bust WR3/flex with legitimate bust risk given 2025 usage and durability questions.


**Three-way battle for the WR3 role: rookies Ja'Kobi Lane and Elijah Sarratt vs. Devontez Walker**

Third-round rookie Ja'Kobi Lane (6'4", USC) has been the standout of camp, making highlight contested catches (including one over Malaki Starks and Marlon Humphrey) and is being described as the leading candidate for the No. 3 WR job, aided by increased 11-personnel usage. He's praised by both HC Jesse Minter and Zay Flowers for quick learning. He's competing with fellow rookie Elijah Sarratt (polished route runner) and third-year deep threat Devontez Walker (projected ~44 catches, 7 TD if he wins the job). Depth options LaJohntay Wester, Cornelius Johnson, Dayton Wade and Xavier Guillory are also in the mix for roster spots.

*Fantasy relevance:* Lane is the rookie WR earning the most real buzz/role in camp and is worth a late-round dynasty/best-ball speculative pick; Walker has the highest per-target upside if he wins snaps; none profile as more than deep-league fantasy considerations yet.


**Isaiah Likely traded away — Mark Andrews is now the clear lone starting TE, rookies fill the void**

Isaiah Likely signed with the New York Giants in free agency, and depth TE Charlie Kolar also left (reportedly to the Chargers), clearing out the Ravens' TE room behind Mark Andrews. Andrews enters his 9th season as the unquestioned starter/veteran leader and has never had fewer than 45 catches/5 TDs in a season; some outlets are calling him a 2026 breakout/bounce-back candidate under the new offense. Behind him, the job is wide open among 5th-round rookie Josh Cuevas, 4th-round rookie Matt Hibner, blocking-specialist veteran Durham Smythe, and Ty Pezza.

*Fantasy relevance:* Andrews is a clear TE1 fantasy target with a full share of red-zone/receiving TE work now that Likely is gone; the rookie TEs are unproven camp battle names with no real fantasy relevance yet.


**Offensive line has real turnover — three new starters, open center competition**

Pro Bowl center Tyler Linderbaum departed in free agency, opening a center battle among veteran Ethan Pocic (seen as the frontrunner), Danny Pinter, Jovaughn Gwyn, and Corey Bullock (plus UDFA Nick Dawkins in the mix). First-round rookie Vega Ioane projects as the new starting right guard, John Simpson returns for a second stint at left guard, Ronnie Stanley remains at left tackle (recently extended), and Roger Rosengarten enters year three at right tackle. The Ravens expect three new line starters in 2026 compared to last season.

*Fantasy relevance:* O-line instability (new center, new RG) is a mild yellow flag for the run game/pass protection consistency early in the season, worth monitoring in preseason snaps.


**2026 PUP list is defense-heavy; no offensive skill players sidelined**

Six Ravens started camp on the PUP list, but all are defensive players: DT Nnamdi Madubuike (neck surgery, since cleared to practice), DT Travis Jones, DT John Jenkins, OLB Adisa Isaac, CB Bilhal Kone, and rookie ILB Teddye Buchanan (knee). No Ravens offensive skill-position players (RB/WR/TE/QB) or offensive linemen were reported on PUP/injured lists as camp opened.

*Fantasy relevance:* No offensive injury-driven opportunity changes to note yet; the RB/WR/TE/OL competitions described above are healthy, natural roster battles rather than injury-driven vacancies.

## Carolina Panthers Offense — 2026 Training Camp / Offseason Fantasy Report

**QB: Bryce Young entrenched as starter, no camp competition, but no extension yet**

Bryce Young is the undisputed QB1 with zero competition for the job. Carolina exercised his 5th-year option in April, tying him to the team through 2027 at ~$25.9M, but EVP Brandt Tilis said the front office wants to "see how the year plays out" before discussing a long-term extension — this is a prove-it Year 4 for Young. Camp reports are notably positive: coach Dave Canales says Young is "playing so much faster" and "eliminating things before the snap," and multiple outlets (RotoBaller, Yahoo, Athlon, CatCrave) describe him as turning heads with better decision-making and chemistry with Tetairoa McMillan. *Fantasy relevance:* Young is a late-round/streaming QB2 with modest weekly ceiling; his camp buzz suggests a slightly improved efficiency profile, but he carries real long-term job-security uncertainty if 2026 doesn't go well. Rico Dowdle, who had emerged as Carolina's most productive back down the stretch in 2025 (23-206 rushing line in one Week 5 explosion, per SI/Onsi retrospectives), left in free agency and signed with the Pittsburgh Steelers. In his place, Carolina signed veteran power back AJ Dillon (ex-Packers/Eagles) to handle short-yardage/goal-line work. Chuba Hubbard — signed to a 4-year, $33.2M extension — remains the depth-chart RB1 and is expected to open the season as the lead back, but with a reduced third-down/short-yardage role now that Dillon is here. The real buzz belongs to Jonathon Brooks (2024 2nd-round pick out of Texas), who is finally healthy after back-to-back ACL tears (one at entry to the league, one three games into his 2025 rookie season) and looked explosive in his first full padded camp; Canales said "he looks fast, he looks like the guy I remember." *Fantasy relevance:* This is a muddled committee, not a bell-cow situation: Hubbard is a low-end RB2/flex to start the year but shares passing-down and goal-line work; Brooks is one of the best buy-low dynasty/best-ball stashes in fantasy if he proves durable, with real path to a 1B or even lead role; Dillon is a touchdown-dependent flex/DFS play in short yardage; Etienne has minimal standalone value. Handcuff priority for Hubbard is arguably Brooks over Dillon given talent, but health flags remain.

Tetairoa McMillan (2025 No. 8 overall pick) is the unquestioned WR1 and has been the standout of camp — one-handed grabs, praised by an NFL insider (via Jordan Schultz) as "even better than advertised," with Canales highlighting his high-point ball skills. Xavier Legette (2024 1st-rounder) is penciled in as the WR3/likely starter opposite them, but he was carted off practice on Aug. 4, 2026 after a hard fall attempting an end-zone catch; he was cleared of a concussion (stinger diagnosis per Canales) and is expected back for the regular season but will miss the Hall of Fame Game. ## Chicago Bears Offense - 2026 Training Camp / Offseason Fantasy Football Report

In a trade finalized at the start of the new league year, Chicago sent DJ Moore (plus a 2026 5th-round pick) to Buffalo for a 2026 2nd-round pick. The move saved the Bears $16.5M in cap space ($12M in dead money) after Moore had a career-low year (50-682-6) in Ben Johnson's first season running the offense. **Rome Odunze is the presumptive WR1, fully healthy after a foot scare, teams project a breakout**

Some fantasy content flagged Odunze with a stress fracture/week-to-week foot issue, but Odunze and beat reporters (Bleacher Nation, BearGoggles On) directly refuted that heading into camp — he said 'My foot feels great... I'm completely fine' and has been full-go at training camp (late July 2026 reports). Before an injury cut short his 2025 season, he was pacing for 1,000+ yards. With Moore gone, he projects as the clear top target earner in Ben Johnson's offense; ESPN's early season projection had him around 118 targets/59-1,029-8.

*Fantasy relevance:* Treat Odunze as a locked-in WR1/2 fantasy target for 2026, not an injury-report concern — but verify the very latest practice reports close to your draft since conflicting 'stress fracture' pieces circulated (likely stale/recycled content).


**Luther Burden III is the clear breakout/sleeper candidate at WR2, working primarily out of the slot**

## Cincinnati Bengals Offense — 2026 Training Camp / Fantasy Football Notes

**QB: Joe Burrow is fully healthy, no QB1 competition; Joe Flacco re-signed as backup**

Burrow enters his 7th season as the unquestioned starter after being named to his 3rd Pro Bowl. He missed 9 games in 2025 with a Grade 3 turf toe injury but returned to throw for 1,809 yds/17 TD/5 INT in his final 8 starts, and multiple camp reports (Fox Sports' Ralph Vacchiano, bengals.com) describe him as fully healthy and 'looking healthy coming into the offseason.' Backup Jake Browning left in free agency (signed with Tampa Bay to back up Baker Mayfield); the Bengals re-signed veteran Joe Flacco, who made his own first Pro Bowl filling in for Burrow last year, as the clear QB2. Josh Johnson and Sean Clifford round out camp depth with no real competition for reps.

*Fantasy relevance:* Burrow is a locked-in top-3 fantasy QB target if you draft him; his return to health (plus an improved defense/roster around him) is the single biggest positive storyline for the whole offense's outlook.


**RB: Chase Brown is a clear bell-cow, not part of a committee**

Chase Brown is described as the 'undisputed starter' and 'indisputable lead' back after eclipsing 1,000 rushing yards last season. He's reportedly in extension talks with the team (with Burrow publicly pushing for the Bengals to sign him long-term) as he enters a contract year. Samaje Perine remains the clear-cut backup/change-of-pace/passing-down piece. Rookie 6th-round pick Tahj Brooks is getting an unusually heavy workload across all three practice units this camp and is viewed as the primary backup/handcuff, with Zac Taylor praising his vision, receiving, and pass-pro ability. Gary Brightwell, Kendall Milton, and UDFAs Jamal Haynes/Kentrell Bullock are competing for one remaining depth spot; only 3 RBs (Brown, Perine, Brooks) are projected to make the 53-man roster.

*Fantasy relevance:* Brown projects as a true 3-down bell-cow back with strong volume upside — a clear winner. Perine has standalone value only in a Brown-injury scenario; Brooks is the top backfield handcuff/stash for Brown drafters.


**WR: Chase and Higgins entrenched 1-2; real battle is at WR3 between Andrei Iosivas and rookie Colbie Young**

Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins are unquestioned as the top two, both considered borderline WR1-caliber options. The competition to watch is the slot/WR3 job: incumbent Andrei Iosivas (entering a contract year) has reportedly had 'a strong start to camp' and is praised by Burrow ('Andrei looks unbelievable... playing with so much confidence') and by Cincy Jungle for improved speed/separation, giving him the current edge. But 4th-round rookie Colbie Young (4.49 speed) is 'actively challenging for first-team reps' and OC Dan Pitcher has praised his upside. Mitchell Tinsley is the next man off the bench behind the top group, with Charlie Jones, Ke'Shawn Williams, and Dohnte Myers battling for remaining depth spots. No major injuries reported to the top-3 receivers.

*Fantasy relevance:* Chase and Higgins remain locked-in weekly fantasy starters. Iosivas currently has the edge for WR3/flex snaps and late-round dart-throw appeal, but Young is a real threat to steal the role/looks if he keeps outperforming in camp — worth monitoring preseason reps.


**TE: Mike Gesicki remains TE1 but Bengals added veteran Noah Fant as TE2, squeezing Erick All and Tanner Hudson**

Mike Gesicki (65 rec/665 yds/2 TD, TE13 in PPR in 2024) is again the top receiving tight end, working more as a detached 'big slot' weapon, while Drew Sample continues as the top in-line blocking TE. In free agency the Bengals signed veteran Noah Fant (released by Seattle) as a TE2, which slightly caps Gesicki's two-TE-package/red-zone upside but doesn't threaten his lead role. Erick All Jr., who missed all of 2025 recovering from a torn ACL (and was separately struck by a vehicle in Greece this offseason but is reportedly ready), is working back in as a depth/2-TE piece; Tanner Hudson serves as a trusted secondary target for Burrow.

*Fantasy relevance:* Gesicki remains the only tight end here with real weekly fantasy relevance; Fant's arrival is a mild dampener on Gesicki's ceiling but not his role. Fant/All/Hudson have no standalone fantasy value barring injury.


**No offensive skill players on PUP/injury lists to start camp**

The notable PUP-list news out of Bengals camp this summer involves the defensive line (DT B.J. Hill placed on PUP), not the offense. No RB/WR/TE/QB appears on PUP, NFI, or IR lists per current camp reporting.

*Fantasy relevance:* Good news for fantasy drafters — no hidden offensive injury landmines to account for entering camp, beyond Burrow's now-resolved toe injury and All's ACL recovery (both trending positive).


**Rookies with real 2026 roles: RB Tahj Brooks and WR Colbie Young**

Beyond the WR3/handcuff battles described above, Brooks (6th-round 2025 pick) is drawing praise as a three-phase contributor (run/pass-catch/protection) rather than just camp-body depth. Colbie Young (4th-round 2026 pick) is pushing for real first-team slot/WR3 snaps as a rookie. No other offensive rookies are drawing meaningful fantasy-relevant camp buzz yet.

*Fantasy relevance:* Brooks is the top late-round/undrafted stash as Chase Brown's direct backup; Young is a deep-dynasty/best-ball WR4 flier worth watching through preseason.

## Los Angeles Chargers 2026 Training Camp / Offseason Offense Report (Fantasy Football)

He made his 6th consecutive NFL Top-100 list (ranked 39th). No injury concerns reported; his offseason throwing volume was intentionally reduced to preserve his arm/body for the season.

*Fantasy relevance:* No QB competition at all — Herbert is a locked-in QB1/streaming-upside play, and the new scheme plus better O-line health (see below) raises his fantasy floor and ceiling for 2026.
