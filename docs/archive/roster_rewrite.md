**Outlook/Keeper Value Change:** **No change — reaffirmed as an elite, must-keep asset.** Full health, no holdout, unchanged alpha role, and a scoring format that plays directly to his strengths make him an easy top-5-overall keeper.

## Bo Nix (QB, Denver Broncos)

**Latest News:** Nix fractured his ankle in Denver's 2025 playoff run but is "on track" for the Week 1 opener at Kansas City, with the team calling recovery "encouraging" and Nix himself saying he feels "normal" and intends to keep his rushing ability intact. The rushing profile plus first-down-bonus scoring and an upgraded passing offense (Waddle/Engram) point toward a positive efficiency bump. A crowded RB committee likely funnels more rushing TDs/first downs through Nix rather than a bell-cow back.

**Outlook/Keeper Value Change:** **Slight positive trend.** Health is trending toward clearance, weapons are upgraded, and the format (Superflex + first-down bonus) plays to his rushing floor — a good QB2/high-end QB1 keep if cost is reasonable. Monitor the Payton-to-Webb play-calling change and Waddle's minor camp tightness issue.

## Mike Evans (WR, San Francisco 49ers)

**Latest News:** Major update — Evans is no longer with Tampa Bay. He signed a 3-year, $42M free-agent deal with the San Francisco 49ers this offseason and is now the team's new WR1, dealing with only a minor quad issue in camp. This is the single biggest change on Andrew's roster this cycle: Tampa Bay's receiving room (Godwin/Egbuka/McMillan) moves on without him, while San Francisco hands Evans a clean WR1 role in Brock Purdy's offense. San Francisco's backfield is banged up around him (Guerendo, James, and rookie Black all simultaneously injured behind Christian McCaffrey), and TE George Kittle is starting camp on PUP recovering from a torn Achilles — both factors that could funnel additional red-zone and early-down passing volume toward the receivers, including Evans.

**Fantasy Impact:** The context change is significant. Evans' contested-catch, red-zone-TD-dependent profile now slots into a new offense as the clear top target, replacing a crowded, target-split Tampa Bay room with a fresh WR1 opportunity in San Francisco. That said, a new team, new QB (Purdy), and new scheme at age 33 (turns 33 this month) carries real adjustment/chemistry risk, and Kittle's eventual return could reclaim some red-zone share. The first-down-bonus format still mutes his value somewhat relative to high-volume possession receivers, but a clean WR1 role with a competent QB is a real asset.

**Outlook/Keeper Value Change:** **Upgrade from the "caution flag" state — new team, but a clear WR1 role.** The uncertainty is no longer about depth-chart standing (that's resolved and favorable) but about scheme fit and age-33 durability. Worth keeping at a moderate WR2 price; track early-camp/preseason chemistry reports with Purdy before locking in a higher valuation.

Dontayvion Wicks (acquired via trade, extended for $12.5M) has emerged as the clear WR2/3 leader ahead of Hollywood Brown and Elijah Moore, giving Smith a much less contested target hierarchy than in past years. His possession-receiver, chain-moving skill set is a strong fit for the first-down-bonus format, and as the new No. 1 option he should see a meaningful target-volume bump even within Philadelphia's run-first (Hurts/Barkley) offense. His recurring hamstring/soft-tissue injury history remains the top durability risk to monitor.

**Outlook/Keeper Value Change:** **Significant upgrade — from capped WR2 to Philadelphia's clear WR1/target leader.** This is a real value jump following the Brown trade; his price should move up accordingly, tempered by the ever-present injury-history flag and Philadelphia's still run-centric offensive identity.
