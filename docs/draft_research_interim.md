## QB (Superflex)

### Tier 1 — True 1a/1b, format-turbocharged
**Players:** Josh Allen (BUF), Lamar Jackson (BAL)

Both are the two best rushing QBs in football and this format's 0.5/rushing-first-down bonus is tailor-made for them — Allen has hit 500+ rush yards with double-digit rush TDs three straight years (constant goal-line/short-yardage conversions = free first downs), and Jackson's scramble-and-slide game racks up FDs even in a down efficiency year. Both go 1-1/1-2 off the board.

*Format note:* MASSIVE RISER for both — arguably the two biggest format risers at the position league-wide.

### Tier 2 — Elite with real format ceiling
**Players:** Drake Maye (NE), Jayden Daniels (WAS), Jalen Hurts (PHI)

Maye broke out as an efficient, ascending passer (72% comp, 113.5 rating) who also added 450 rush yards/4 rush TDs. Daniels is a true dual-threat when healthy (injury availability is the only knock). Hurts remains a short-yardage/tush-push/red-zone rushing machine even in a 'down' rushing year (421 yds/8 TDs) — losing A.J. Brown (traded to Pabst Interference's roster) hurts his passing ceiling but his floor is almost entirely rush-FD-driven, which this format loves.

*Format note:* All three are format risers; Hurts specifically because his value is now MORE concentrated in the exact stat (rushing FDs) this league pays extra for.

### Tier 3 — Elite pure passers (format-neutral-to-faller)
**Players:** Joe Burrow (CIN)

Elite volume passer with Chase/Higgins, but zero rushing equity. In a standard 6pt-TD superflex he's a top-3 lock; here the lack of a passing-TD premium and total absence of rush-FD bonus points caps his format ceiling relative to the dual-threats above him.

*Format note:* Notable FALLER relative to generic superflex ADP — still a strong QB1, just not a top-2/3 asset here.

### Tier 4 — High-floor weekly starters
**Players:** Caleb Williams (CHI) — UNAVAILABLE, kept by Glizzy Guzzler, Trevor Lawrence (JAC), Justin Herbert (LAC), Dak Prescott (DAL), Brock Purdy (SF)

Williams is genuinely a riser (rushing ability, ascending under Ben Johnson) but off Andrew's board entirely as a confirmed keeper elsewhere. Lawrence is the real story: closed 2025 at 26.6 PPG over his last 7 games with 359 rush yards/9 rush TDs under new OC Liam Coen's scheme — a legitimate rushing profile that didn't exist before. Herbert, Prescott, and Purdy are all excellent but essentially non-runners, so their format ceiling is capped versus the names above them.

*Format note:* Lawrence is a clear RISER (newfound rushing volume); Herbert/Prescott/Purdy are roughly format-neutral to slight fallers — great real-life QBs whose fantasy scoring here is capped by zero rush-FD equity.

### Tier 5 — QB2 value with spike-week ceilings
**Players:** Jaxson Dart (NYG) — UNAVAILABLE, kept by Glizzy Guzzler, Bo Nix (DEN) — Andrew's own keeper, confirmed healthy, Patrick Mahomes (KC), Kyler Murray (MIN)

Dart flashed a real rushing floor as a rookie (487 yds/9 rush TDs) but is off the board. Nix is fully cleared from his ankle procedures ('nothing's holding me back') and remains an efficient game-manager with a real scrambling floor — a great value at his R12 keeper cost. Mahomes is coming off ACL/LCL surgery with his mobility compromised and Week 1 status still a question mark — a real name at a discount if he slips, but the injury risk plus reduced rushing burst make him a tier below his career norm. Murray landed with Kevin O'Connell in Minnesota and is the presumptive favorite over J.J. McCarthy — massive rushing-FD upside if he wins and holds the job.

*Format note:* Nix and Murray are format RISERS (rushing floor); Mahomes is a relative FALLER versus his historical ranking because part of his edge (extending plays with his legs) is compromised post-injury, on top of pure recovery risk.

### Tier 6 — Streamers / cheap-but-startable (the format's real bargain bin)
**Players:** Tyler Shough (NO), Cam Ward (TEN), Sam Darnold (SEA), Baker Mayfield (TB), C.J. Stroud (HOU), Malik Willis (MIA), Bryce Young (CAR)

Shough was roughly QB12-pace points-per-game from Week 9 on as a rookie — a legit streamer. Ward improved dramatically late (15.5 PPG over his final 4 starts) and has real running ability. Willis is the sleakiest format-specific value: 44.8 rush yards per game in his starts — in a league that pays 0.5 per rushing first down, a low-ceiling passer who runs constantly can out-score far more 'talented' pocket passers on a per-game basis if he's ever a full-time starter. Young and Stroud are boom/bust names hoping for better surroundings.

*Format note:* Willis, Ward, and Shough are the clearest FORMAT RISERS in this tier — cheap, late-round, and their fantasy value is disproportionately tied to rushing volume that this scoring system pays extra for. This is exactly the tier to stream from off waivers rather than roster all season.

### Tier 7 — Deep/desperation SUPER_FLEX fills and stashes
**Players:** Jared Goff (DET), Matthew Stafford (LAR), Jordan Love (GB), Geno Smith (NYJ), Daniel Jones (IND), Jacoby Brissett (ARI), Aaron Rodgers (PIT), Fernando Mendoza (LV) — rookie stash, Justin Fields (KC) — Mahomes handcuff stash, Anthony Richardson Sr. (IND) — buried but rushing-upside stash

Goff and Stafford are good real QBs but pure pocket passers with zero rush equity — in a 6pt-TD league they'd be more attractive; here they're pure QB2/desperation SUPER_FLEX fills only. Jones just signed as Colts' 'unquestioned QB1' recovering from Achilles surgery and historically has rushing juice if fully healthy — worth a very late look. Mendoza (No. 1 overall pick) is stuck behind Kirk Cousins to open camp but is a dual-threat prospect worth a dynasty-style stash pick very late. Fields is arguably the single best 'if he plays' asset in this entire tier — he's Mahomes' backup in KC after a trade from NYJ, and if Mahomes' knee doesn't hold, Fields' rushing floor would instantly make him a top-15 format asset. Richardson lost the Colts job to Jones and is fighting Riley Leonard just to be the primary backup, but the rushing ceiling (if health and QB1 job ever align) is real.

*Format note:* Goff/Stafford/Rodgers/Brissett are clear FALLERS in this format (no rushing equity at all); Fields, Mendoza, Jones, and Richardson are speculative RISERS purely on rushing-first-down upside if a path to playing time opens.

**Risers in this format:** Josh Allen — elite floor AND ceiling from rushing FDs, format's biggest riser; Lamar Jackson — same profile as Allen, buy-low after a down 2025; Trevor Lawrence — newfound rushing volume (359 yds/9 rush TDs) under Liam Coen changes his profile entirely; Jalen Hurts — value now concentrated almost entirely in the exact stat (short-yardage rush FDs) this league pays extra for; Drake Maye / Jayden Daniels — dual-threat ascension, both gain extra points nobody else's league-mates are gaining; Caleb Williams / Jaxson Dart — legit format risers nationally, but note: BOTH are off Andrew's board (kept by Glizzy Guzzler); Kyler Murray — if he wins the Vikings job under Kevin O'Connell, instant rushing-FD value; Bo Nix — Andrew's own R12 keeper; confirmed healthy, decent scrambling floor, plus format friendliness makes that keeper cost look better than a generic ranking suggests; Malik Willis / Cam Ward / Tyler Shough — cheap, late-round streamers whose fantasy relevance is driven almost entirely by rushing attempts, exactly what this format overpays for; Justin Fields (KC) and Fernando Mendoza (LV) — deep bench stashes with outsized upside if a starting path opens, because their calling card (legs) is the format's premium stat

**Fallers in this format:** Joe Burrow — elite passer but zero rush equity; no 6pt-TD premium here either, so he grades below typical superflex consensus; Jared Goff — excellent real-life QB, but total lack of rushing means he gets none of the FD bonus; pure QB2 in this format; Matthew Stafford / Dak Prescott / Justin Herbert — all pocket passers whose fantasy ceiling is capped without a rushing floor; Aaron Rodgers / Geno Smith / Jacoby Brissett — aging or limited pocket passers, near-replacement level here; Patrick Mahomes — still talented, but ACL/LCL recovery specifically compromises the scrambling/extending-plays part of his game that used to add incremental value; also outright missed-time risk

Cliffs for draft strategy: (1) A sharp cliff after Allen/Jackson — nobody else offers that rushing floor + passing ceiling combo. (2) A second cliff after the Maye/Daniels/Hurts/Burrow group (~QB6) — from QB7 on you're picking a 'great but flawed' starter (injury risk, no legs, or scheme uncertainty). (3) Because this is a 10-team league with exactly 20 SUPER_FLEX-eligible starting slots, QB20ish is real replacement level — meaning Tier 6 streamers (Shough, Ward, Willis, Darnold) are legitimately startable-and-cheap; don't reach for a Goff/Stafford-type pocket passer late when a Willis/Ward-type rushing streamer scores similarly here for a fraction of the draft cost. (4) Andrew already has Bo Nix locked in as a strong-value SUPER_FLEX QB1/1B at a R12 keeper price — his draft priority should be finding a complementary QB in the Tier 3-5 range (Lawrence, Herbert, Purdy, Murray, or a Mahomes-discount) with his early/mid picks (R1=8, R2=13, R3=28) rather than reaching for a third early QB, since Caleb Williams and Jaxson Dart (two of the best format fits in Tier 3/4) are already off the board via Glizzy Guzzler's keepers, thinning the realistic target list at that value point.

KEY SCORING-FORMAT PRINCIPLE: 0.5 PPR + 0.5/first down (rush AND rec) rewards (a) every-down volume backs who rack up both rushing and receiving first downs, (b) high-efficiency/high-target pass-catching backs whose catches are schemed as high-percentage, chain-moving throws (checkdowns, screens, angle routes) rather than low-percentage/low-catch-rate deep looks, and (c) grinding, positive-EPA rushers who consistently pick up 4+ yards (more likely to hit a first down marker) over long-TD/boom-bust rushers whose per-touch value is inflated in standard leagues by explosive-play equity that doesn't repeat as reliably. It punishes committee/timeshare backs (touches split = fewer total first downs) and pure early-down/goal-line role players with no receiving work. Superflex context: with 20 combined starting slots at QB and only 12 realistic bell-cow/lead-back RBs plus real committees everywhere, elite/high-volume workhorse RBs (Gibbs, Bijan, McCaffrey, Taylor, Chase Brown, Cook, Jeanty, Walker) should be drafted very aggressively relative to a standard superflex-only strategy — RB scarcity is severe and this scoring format widens, rather than narrows, the gap between true workhorses and shared backfields.

### Tier 6 — Streaming Defenses (draft R15-16 or punt to Week 1 waivers)
**Players:** Houston Texans, Seattle Seahawks, Los Angeles Rams, Denver Broncos (schedule risk), Detroit Lions (early-season stream), Baltimore Ravens (early-season stream), Los Angeles Chargers (early-season stream)

Houston projects as the No. 1 DST after finishing DST2 in 2025 (3rd-fewest points allowed/game) — the most 'safe' unit to actually roster full-time. Seattle was the highest-scoring DST in the league last year (17.2 PPG allowed) under Mike Macdonald and opens against Patriots/Cardinals. The Rams added Myles Garrett to a unit already 4th in pressure rate and upgraded the secondary. Denver grades out elite on paper but carries the 3rd-toughest schedule in the first 8 weeks plus regression risk after a career year, so treat it as a talent play, not a schedule play. For the streaming approach specifically: Detroit has the NFL's easiest schedule (especially weeks 1-5), Baltimore has the 2nd-easiest first six weeks under new DC Jesse Minter, and the Chargers open with Arizona and Las Vegas — all plug-in-play Week 1-3 streamers even if you don't roster them long-term.

*Format note:* DST scoring (sacks/turnovers/points-allowed tiers) is essentially unaffected by the receiving-first-down rule change — this is the one category where the league's scoring quirks don't move the needle, so draft-capital logic reverts to standard theory.

**Risers in this format:** Slot/possession WRs with low aDOT and high target share (Jalen Nailor, Antonio Williams, Jaylin Noel, Jayden Higgins, Jalen Coker) — every short completion that gains 5+ yards nets an extra 0.5 pt on top of the 0.5 PPR, so high-floor chain-movers outscore their raw yardage/TD stat lines.; Possession/seam tight ends who work underneath and up the seam (Travis Kelce, Mark Andrews, Brenton Strange, Greg Dulcich) — same logic, amplified because TEs run a disproportionate share of high-percentage, chain-moving routes.; Pass-catching handcuff/committee RBs (Justice Hill, Jordan James, Samaje Perine, Jordan Mason) — check-down/screen roles convert into first downs at a high rate even on low-aDOT, low-YAC catches.; Mobile backup/streamer QBs (Kyler Murray, Malik Willis) — designed QB runs and scrambles that pick up first downs are a hidden bonus category most redraft rankings don't price in.

**Fallers in this format:** Boom-bust deep threats and big-play-dependent WRs (Denzel Boston's vertical-only profile, Tre Harris's contested-deep-ball archetype) — a 45-yard catch only earns one first-down bonus just like a 6-yard catch does, so per-target efficiency actually favors possession guys over field-stretchers.; TD-or-bust rookie/committee RBs without receiving work (Tank Bigsby, Chris Brooks-style short-yardage/power-only handcuffs) — they miss out on the receiving half of the first-down bonus entirely, capping their weekly ceiling relative to pass-catching handcuffs at the same draft cost.; Ryan Flournoy — flagged as a sleeper, but his one notable game was TD/big-play driven (9-115-1); in this scoring system he's more boom/bust than his ADP-value profile suggests unless his target share becomes truly reliable.; Pure garbage-time/desperation-heave WRs and any player whose fantasy case rests on touchdown regression alone — TD-dependent scorers are explicitly punished relative to possession players under this rule set, so be skeptical of sleeper profiles pitched purely on 'he'll score more TDs.'

Why DEF should be drafted dead last: (1) week-to-week DST scoring is driven by sacks/turnovers/return TDs, which are highly variable and poorly predicted by preseason unit rankings — research consistently shows streaming by weekly matchup outperforms rostering one 'elite' defense all year; (2) with 7 of 10 starting slots RB/WR/TE-eligible and a real SUPERFLEX QB scarcity problem (20 QB slots, ~10-12 truly startable QBs plus backups), every one of Andrew's picks through round 14 is better spent on a skill player or QB2 than on a defense; (3) Andrew specifically has acute needs (RB2, WR2, TE, 2x FLEX, only 1 RB/1 WR rostered after keepers) and two 19-pick dead zones (108->128, 128->148) where he can't afford to burn a pick on DEF when a skill-position swing player might still be sitting there. Recommendation: take a defense with one of his final two picks (R15 pick 148 or R16 pick 153 — after locking up his skill-position bench), targeting Houston, Seattle, or a Detroit/Baltimore/Chargers early-schedule play, and treat Week 1 waivers as the real Plan A/backup for defense.

Also flag for Andrew: several 'available sleeper' names on general fantasy sites are actually NOT available in this specific league because they're already locked as keepers by opponents — Jaydon Blue (RB, DAL) is kept by PeterCrisileo (R15 cost); Bucky Irving, Dalton Kincaid, and Tucker Kraft are all locked to jomud (R12 cost each); Brock Bowers is locked to PeterCrisileo (R8). Double-check any late-round target against the keeper board before building draft rankings.
